## Readout and matching checks worth doing before execution

1. **Capture both role spaces at layer 16.** SUCA_L16 reproduces the paper's four-role diagnostic, but this adaptation introduces Tool text, which that classifier does not distinguish. Also project the same layer-16 states through SUCAT_L16, in addition to the planned SUCAT_L12 manipulation check. This requires no extra forward passes. Retaining both enables a faithful paper comparison and a Tool-aware diagnostic without relabeling Tool data as User. If the five-role layer-16 asset is unavailable, record that explicitly and keep four-role CoTness claims narrow.
2. **Align each probability with the token processed by that forward call.** An activation at position t belongs to the input token at t, not the next sampled token. The terminal sampled token may never receive its own forward pass. Preserve absolute positions, turn IDs and span offsets; do not manufacture a final-token reading or reuse duplicate prefill states across turns. Saved online probabilities are sufficient and avoid storing full hidden-state tensors.
3. **Keep steering and readout hooks explicit.** The edit remains block-11 output, on tool-content prompt tokens only; layer-12 and layer-16 normalized readouts are downstream. Keep decoding tokens unedited. When a tool span reappears in a later full prompt, reapply the same mask convention. Record edited-token counts and mask-boundary checks for every call.
4. **Freeze all arm inputs and runtime settings.** Keep seed 1239, source hashes, model/tokenizer revision, vector/probe hashes, sampling configuration, stopping rules and sandbox configuration. The initial pre-fetch prompts should match; the styled-versus-steered after-fetch prompts must be byte-identical. The other arms differ only in their declared payload span. Preserve the historical record separately from fresh outcomes.
5. **Keep behavioral evidence independent of probe movement.** Report the receiver-verified dummy upload, attempted upload, exposure, censoring and final summary for each arm. A low CoTness trace is not itself a blocked attack; a refusal alone is not necessarily successful summary completion. Do not mark an unexposed or censored run as a protected example.
