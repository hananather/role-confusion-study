# Learning from an adverse result

Authored illustrative conversation. These passages were supplied as model input; they are not a real exchange with Neel or model-generated reasoning.

## User 1

Hi! I'm writing up a MATS project to discuss with Neel. Suppose a steering vector moves the role-probe score strongly, but the assistant still follows the unwanted instruction. What would be the most useful way to investigate and present that result?

## CoT 1

The user describes a mismatch between a measurement and the behavior it was meant to predict. I should treat this as an informative result rather than a failure to decorate with a positive story. First, the actual intervention and behavioral scoring need verification. If those checks pass, the readout may not be sufficient for the intended behavioral change, or the intervention may affect the wrong location or time. The answer should recommend a few discriminating checks and make the observed dissociation visible in the report without claiming that every role representation is behaviorally irrelevant.

## Assistant 1

Verify that the intended activation edit was applied and inspect the complete generated answers using a fixed behavioral rule. Then show the readout change and behavioral outcome together. The immediate finding is that this intervention changed this probe score without producing the desired behavior in the tested cases. Useful follow-ups include moving the intervention to a justified alternative location, varying its timing, and comparing with a simple instruction baseline. Preserve the original result even if a later setting works. It identifies a limitation of the current measurement-intervention pair and helps prevent a strong internal score shift from being mistaken for a successful behavioral control.

## User 2

Thanks! How can I make that finding the center of the MATS report without sounding as though the project was only a collection of things that did not work? I'd like the reader to leave with a concrete lesson.

## CoT 2

The user wants a narrative organized around what was learned rather than the chronology of attempts. I should state the relationship being tested, the evidence that distinguishes two quantities, and the practical consequence for later experiments. The report can be affirmative about a demonstrated dissociation while remaining narrow about its scope. A side-by-side figure gives the lesson a concrete anchor. The answer should avoid universal claims from a small case set and explain why preserving the adverse result improves the research direction rather than merely satisfying a reporting convention.

## Assistant 2

Build the report around the distinction the experiment reveals. Begin with the intended link between the role readout and instruction-following behavior. Then show the intervention, the measured score change, and the unchanged or undesirable behavior in the same figure or table. Explain what that means for the next research decision: the current probe score is insufficient as the sole success criterion for this intervention. State the tested scope and the checks that make the observation credible. Follow with one experiment that could discriminate among the remaining explanations. This gives the report a concrete lesson and a reasoned next step, even when the original intervention falls short.
